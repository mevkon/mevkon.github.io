01 — OVERVIEW

The Brief

Dominator is a store specialising in high-performance gaming PCs and custom builds. The old identity didn’t match the product — it read generic, with no recognisable mark and no system to hold the brand together across print and retail.

The goal was a flexible identity that feels native to gaming culture, scales from a business card to a building facade, and gives the store a consistent voice across every touchpoint.

The Idea

I’ve built and tinkered with PCs since childhood, so the category is close to me. The concept landed on a bionic processor — technology grown like an organism.

That tension drives every decision: organic, flowing logo shapes against angular, machine-cut typography, wrapped in an electric acid-green that signals raw performance.

03 — LOGO

A Bionic Core

The mark reads as a sphere woven from organic, liquid strands — a planet, a cell, a chip seen under a microscope. It’s the fusion of nature and technology that names the whole system. The primary lockup is versatile: it holds up on any background and in any colour scheme while staying instantly recognisable.

From sketch to symbol

The logo evolved from a simple core into a complex form. At each stage strands were added to symbolise the growing fusion of hardware and biology, ending in a dense, dynamic mark.

How the symbol is built

For all its organic feel, the mark is constructed on a strict module. A bounding circle sets the silhouette, the strands sit on a shared grid, and a fixed clear-space ring keeps it breathing at any size.

  • Bounding circle defines the outer silhouette and optical balance.
  • Module grid — one unit X derived from the radius. The symbol spans 8X; clear space is 1X on every side.
  • Centre axes keep the woven strands symmetrical around the core.
  • Clear space — one module on every side, never encroached.

Rules that keep it clean

The brandbook locks the do-nots: no distortion or stretching, no outlines added to the mark, never set the symbol smaller than the type, and never place it on a background close to its own colour. Consistent proportions are what keep the identity professional at every size.

One mark, every context

Stripped of the wordmark, the symbol stands on its own — one solid colour, no effects or gradients. It holds full-strength on light and dark, and reverses cleanly out of the brand green or black.

04 — TYPOGRAPHY

Machine Meets Reader

Two roles. J-LOG Rebellion Sans is the wordmark face — a modern, angular display whose hard cuts echo the geometry of hardware, reserved for the logo and the brand name itself. For on-screen and printed headings the system uses Archivo Expanded, a heavy grotesque that reads cleanly at any length, while Helvetica handles body copy so specs and descriptions never fight the brand.

06 — PATTERN

Two Textures, One Origin

The identity carries two supporting patterns, both born from the brand itself. They add surface and rhythm without ever fighting the logo — one as structure, one as texture.

Circuit Trace
PCB-style routing lines. Used as accent bands, edges and dividers — a literal nod to the hardware inside.
Symbol Texture
The mark tiled into a tonal field. A quiet, branded backdrop for cards, packaging and large surfaces.

Where each one lives

The two patterns split the work cleanly — the trace draws the eye along edges and seams, the texture fills calm areas with brand presence.
